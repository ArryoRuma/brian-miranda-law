# Brian Miranda Law — Design Direction

## Three stylistic approaches

### Theme Name: Quiet Authority
Very restrained, editorial, and human; a law practice that feels steady, considered, and quietly premium.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Civic Modernism
Structured, architectural, and civic-minded; a contemporary legal office with strong wayfinding and confident geometry.
Probability: 0.03

### Theme Name: Heirloom Ledger
Warm, tactile, and archival; estate planning expressed through the visual language of family documents, ledgers, and enduring materials.
Probability: 0.05

## Chosen approach: Quiet Authority

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial minimalism with the composure of institutional design and the warmth of a private client practice.

### Core Principles
1. Use restraint as a signal of trust: fewer elements, more intentional spacing, and strong typographic hierarchy.
2. Balance dark, grounded surfaces with warm paper tones so the site feels credible without becoming cold.
3. Create an asymmetric reading rhythm that moves from clear guidance to personal reassurance.
4. Make every interaction feel deliberate, quiet, and easy to understand.

### Color Philosophy
Graphite and gunmetal provide dependable legal gravitas. Dark slate grey and dark teal introduce calm, human confidence. Floral white and sand dune make the experience feel like a well-made letter or archival paper. Dark goldenrod acts as the signature brass accent for attention and continuity. Chocolate brown and brandy are reserved for small moments of warmth and emphasis, never as decoration for its own sake.

### Layout Paradigm
A left-anchored editorial composition with a persistent vertical identity rail, wide text columns, and offset content blocks. The page should feel composed like a thoughtful brief rather than a marketing grid.

### Signature Elements
- A slim brass rule and monogram-like legal mark used as a recurring wayfinding cue.
- Oversized serif section numerals and short eyebrow labels for editorial rhythm.
- Layered paper-toned panels with crisp dark borders, suggesting documents and durable records.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should reassure rather than entertain. Hover states reveal intent through small shifts in underline, brass rule, or icon position. CTAs should be direct and low-pressure. Expandable information should feel like opening a well-organized file, never like a flashy modal.

### Animation
Use subtle entrance fades and 12–20px vertical reveals on section content, staggered by 50ms. Keep transitions under 260ms with a snappy ease-out. Buttons compress slightly on press. Avoid looping motion, parallax, or decorative movement. Respect reduced-motion preferences.

### Typography System
Use DM Serif Display for high-contrast headlines and editorial section titles, paired with Manrope for navigation, body copy, labels, and buttons. Headlines should use generous line-height and occasional italic emphasis; body text should remain compact, calm, and highly legible.

### Brand Essence
A steady estate-planning practice for people who want their wishes clearly protected and thoughtfully carried forward. Personality: composed, attentive, enduring.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are plainspoken, specific, and quietly confident. CTAs are invitational, not urgent. Microcopy reduces uncertainty and makes the next step feel manageable.

Example lines:
- “Plan with clarity. Leave with confidence.”
- “A thoughtful plan is one less question for your family.”

### Wordmark & Logo
A compact BM ligature inside a squared archival frame, drawn with a single brass line and a subtle break in the lower-right corner to suggest an open document and continuity.

### Signature Brand Color
Dark goldenrod #a98d3a — used as a restrained brass accent for rules, active states, and key moments of emphasis.

## Style Decisions

- The BM framed mark functions as a recurring desktop margin rail and wayfinding cue, not only as a header logo.
- Brandy and chocolate warmth remain supporting accents; the final conversion area stays grounded in graphite, paper, dark teal, and brass.
- Ledger numerals, crisp borders, slim brass rules, and archival language are repeated across service, quote, process, and FAQ sections.
